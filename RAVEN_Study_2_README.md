# RAVEN Study 2: Predictions and Explanations for Abstract Visual Reasoning

This repository contains the materials, model code, stimuli, anonymized interaction logs, and analysis notebooks for the first RAVEN experiment. The study examined how an AI prediction and two forms of explainable AI (XAI) support affected participants' answers and confidence on abstract visual reasoning problems.

The two explanation conditions were:

1. **LLM explanation:** a prediction and natural-language explanation generated by Claude.
2. **Occlusion Sensitivity (OS):** a prediction generated by a DCNet model and a visual heatmap showing image regions that influenced the model's output.

This repository contains the model code, RAVEN stimuli, participant-facing materials, anonymized interaction logs, model-probability documentation, and analysis notebooks for the second RAVEN experiment.

The study compared five forms of decision support on abstract visual reasoning problems:
1. **Human Only**
2. **Prediction Only**
3. **Prediction + LLM Explanation**
4. **Prediction + Occlusion Sensitivity Heatmap**
5. **Prediction + Predicted Probabilities**

## Study overview

The task used RAVEN-style 3 × 3 abstract visual reasoning matrices. Each matrix contains one missing panel and eight candidate answers. Participants selected the answer that they believed best completed the pattern and rated their confidence.

This was a **single-stage, between-subjects study**:

- **100 participants** were included in the interaction log.
- Participants were assigned to **five conditions**, with **20 participants per condition**.
- Each participant was expected to complete **10 puzzles**.
- The selected AI predictions contained **six correct and four incorrect outcomes**, corresponding to an experimental AI accuracy of **60%** within each AI-assisted condition.
- Participant confidence was recorded on a **1–5 scale**.
- Participants in the LLM, OS, and probability conditions also answered three post-task questions concerning perceived understanding, clarity, and trust.

The conditions were designed to compare unaided human performance with prediction support, explanation support, and direct uncertainty information.

## Experimental conditions and group codes

The interaction log stores the condition in the `ugroup` column.

| Group | Experimental condition | Information shown to the participant | Prediction source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | Human Only | Puzzle and answer choices only | No AI prediction was shown |
| `B` | Prediction Only | AI-selected answer | DCNet |
| `C` | Prediction + LLM Explanation | LLM-selected answer and natural-language rationale | Claude 3.7 Sonnet |
| `D` | Prediction + OS Heatmap | DCNet-selected answer and Occlusion Sensitivity visualization | DCNet |
| `E` | Prediction + Predicted Probabilities | DCNet-selected answer and the softmax distribution over all eight options | DCNet |

### Group F is not an experimental participant group

`results_analysis_experiment.ipynb` creates a synthetic dictionary called `group_f_data` from Group E participants. Group F is a **post-hoc derived policy**, not a sixth randomized condition and not a new set of participants.

## Trial procedure

All participants solved the same set of ten puzzles. The presentation depended on the assigned condition.

A typical trial contained:

1. A RAVEN matrix with one missing panel.
2. Eight possible answer choices.
3. Condition-specific support, when applicable:
   - no support;
   - a highlighted AI answer;
   - an AI answer plus an LLM explanation;
   - an AI answer plus an OS heatmap; or
   - an AI answer plus probabilities for all answer choices.
4. The participant's selected answer.
5. A confidence rating from 1 to 5.
